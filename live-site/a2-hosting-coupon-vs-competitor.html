Is the A2 Hosting coupon actually the best deal, or do competitor coupons win?

The A2 Hosting (now hosting.com) discount is real and auto-applied — but a headline “X% off” is measured against that provider’s own regular rate, which is different from every competitor’s. So the only honest way to tell whether A2 or a rival coupon is cheaper is to compare the absolute monthly $ at checkout and the renewal rate, not the percentages. Coupon aggregators rarely do this. You normally don’t need a third-party code — open hosting.com/hosting/ and the rate applies automatically.

Why a headline “% off” hides the real answer

A percentage discount is a ratio, not a price. It is measured against each provider’s own “regular” rate, and those anchors are not the same across hosts. A page can advertise “up to 75% off A2” while a competitor advertises a similar headline figure for its own plans — yet the real recurring $ you pay can be world apart, because the two “regular” prices they’re discounting from are different.

This is exactly the gap most “A2 Hosting coupon” searchers hit: you came to decide which host to buy, but the coupon pages only argue that their host is cheap. The percentage was never built to answer a comparison question.

The one metric that actually decides it: absolute $ at checkout

Strip the marketing back to a single comparable unit — the recurring dollar amount the cart shows per month, both for the first term and after renewal. Everything else (badges, countdown timers, “best deal” banners) is decoration. The table below is the method to apply at every provider’s own checkout; we don’t publish rival dollar figures here because they change constantly and we won’t guess them.

Renewal is the part that decides the multi-year bill

This is the angle coupon lists skip most. hosting.com’s own renewal page is explicit: “If you choose monthly, or after that prepaid period ends, your website plans will renew at our regular pricing,” and the savings it advertises come “when paying for a year or two or three in advance.” In plain terms, the headline discount is a first-term story; the renewal rate is the multi-year story.

That matters for a comparison because a host with a bigger first-term percentage can still lose over three years if its renewal is steep. The percentage tells you nothing about that. Only the two recurring $ figures at checkout do.

Where DealDrop, HotDeals and TrulyCodes fall short on this question

This is a factual comparison of what those pages do, not a judgment on the companies. The pattern is structural:

  • Single-store by design. Each runs a separate page per provider (their “A2 Hosting” store, their “Hostinger” store, etc.). They are built to sell one host at a time, so they never put two providers’ real checkout $ side by side.
  • They show the % and the code, not the absolute $. You’ll see “up to X% off” and an active-code list, but rarely the recurring monthly $ the cart will actually bill — and almost never the renewal $.
  • They don’t model the multi-year cost. Because renewal isn’t shown, none of them can honestly answer “is A2 cheaper than a rival over three years?”
  • They rarely flag the rebrand. Several still present an “A2 Hosting” identity with legacy code lists, without making clear the discount now lives on hosting.com and that old codes may not validate.

The result: if your real question is “which coupon saves me the most,” those pages can each tell you their host is cheap, but none of them will do the comparison for you. (We don’t link to them from this page.)

6-step checklist: which coupon actually wins for you

  1. Pick your spec first. Decide the plan size you need (sites, storage, region) so you’re comparing like for like across hosts.
  2. Open each provider’s own checkout. Not a coupon page — the cart, where the real recurring $ appears.
  3. Write down first-term $/mo. The number the discount actually produces for the term you’ll buy.
  4. Find the renewal $/mo. On hosting.com that’s the regular rate after the prepaid period; do the same for each rival.
  5. Total it over 12 / 24 / 36 months. First-term total + renewal total for the same window. Highest headline % can lose here.
  6. Subtract what’s bundled. Free migration, free domain, money-back window, and included email change the real cost even when the $ looks close.

If you only ever compare the percentages, you’re comparing marketing units from different anchors. Compare the absolute recurring $ at checkout and the renewal, and the answer is usually unambiguous.

Frequently asked questions

Is the A2 Hosting coupon actually cheaper than a competitor's coupon?

Not necessarily, and the headline percentage can't tell you. A “% off” is measured against that provider's own regular rate, which differs from every rival's. The only honest comparison is the absolute recurring $ shown at each provider's checkout (first term and renewal), not the advertised percentages.

Why doesn't a big '% off' guarantee the cheapest plan?

Because the discount is a ratio of an unstated anchor price. Two hosts can both advertise a similar headline percentage while their real monthly dollar cost differs substantially. The percentage is a marketing unit, not a price.

What should I compare instead of the percentage?

Compare three things at each provider's cart: the first-term recurring $ per month, the renewal $ per month after the prepaid period, and the total you'd pay over 12/24/36 months. hosting.com states plans renew at regular pricing once the prepaid period ends, so the renewal rate dominates the multi-year bill.

Does A2 Hosting (hosting.com) still give an automatic discount?

Yes. hosting.com (previously A2 Hosting) applies a promotional rate automatically at order — no third-party code needed. It also states a 30-day money-back guarantee, free managed migration, free domain name registration, and no lock-ins or minimum terms.

Can I trust a coupon site's 'A2 vs competitor' comparison?

Most single-store coupon pages list codes and a headline % for one provider at a time and rarely show the renewal rate or put two providers' real checkout $ side by side. To answer “is A2 cheaper than a rival” you generally have to open each provider's own checkout and compare the absolute recurring $ yourself.
