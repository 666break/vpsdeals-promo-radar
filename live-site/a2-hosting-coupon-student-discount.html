What students actually search for

When a student types "a2 hosting student discount" or "hosting.com edu coupon," they usually mean one of two things — and the honest answer depends on which they mean:

  • Meaning A — "Is there a special education rate I qualify for?" A discounted price reserved for students / .edu addresses, the way some software does.

The student-relevant value is the freebies, not a code

This is the part coupon lists skip. A "70% off A2 Hosting" headline sounds bigger than "free domain + free migration," but for a student the freebies are often the larger real-world saving:

  • Free domain (year one). Registering a domain normally costs money at a registrar every year. Getting it free with the plan removes a recurring first-year expense — provided you remember the renewal is separate and shop the renewal price later.
  • Free managed migration. If you already have a site, email, or domain elsewhere, the in-house team moves it for free with no per-move fee. That's real time and money saved versus doing it yourself or paying a freelancer.
  • 30-day money-back. A student budget can't absorb a bad purchase. The guarantee makes the first month effectively a no-risk trial — as long as you confirm what's refundable (a registered domain usually isn't fully refundable).
  • No lock-ins. "No hidden fees, no lock-ins or minimum terms" means you aren't trapped in a multi-year contract. Month-to-month is viable if your need is short.

None of these requires a code, and none of them shows up in a coupon site's "% off" number. They're structural, and they apply to students exactly as they apply to everyone else.

How the discount is delivered (same as everyone)

The mechanics are identical for students and non-students, and they explain why a "student code" hunt is usually a dead end:

  • The plan page is the source of truth. hosting.com shows the discounted rate on the page itself — you don't paste a code to see or get it.
  • At checkout the rate is applied automatically. Selecting a plan carries that plan-page promo into the order with no code box to fill for the discount to exist.
  • A code is optional and only useful if it lowers the total. Because the page already discounts, a code only matters if it produces a cart total below the plan-page price for the same plan and term. The cart is where that is proven.
