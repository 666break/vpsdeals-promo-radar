hosting.com's 30-day money-back guarantee is real — but it isn't "everything you paid, no questions asked"

Yes, there is a guarantee. hosting.com (the brand formerly known as A2 Hosting) states a 30-day money-back guarantee on its hosting plans. It is your genuine safety net: if the service, speed or support isn't what you expected — or a renewal price surprises you — you can cancel inside the first 30 days and get your hosting plan fee back. But "money-back" is scoped to the hosting plan; things billed separately (a domain name registration, paid add-ons) are often not refundable, and the exact full-versus-prorated rules live in hosting.com's own Terms of Service. Coupon sites push codes and headline "% off" and almost never explain this — so the real risk-free lever is the guarantee, not the coupon.

What "30-day money-back" does NOT automatically mean (the gap)

This is the part almost no coupon page spells out. A money-back guarantee on a hosting plan is not the same as a refund of your entire order. Why the guarantee is your anti-renewal-shock tool

A first-term coupon looks generous on the order page and then the plan renews at the standard rate — the renewal-price gap we covered in our renewal-price article. The money-back guarantee is what makes that gap survivable: you can cancel inside the first 30 days, before any renewal is ever charged, if the renewal math or the day-to-day service isn't right for you.

Put differently: a coupon code optimises the price you see today. The 30-day guarantee protects the decision you're making for the whole year. For a budget-minded buyer, the guarantee is the more important lever — and it's the one coupon aggregators almost never put next to their codes.

How to actually use the guarantee (the only reliable steps)

  1. Buy on the official plan page, not a resold "deal." Start from hosting.com's own plans so the guarantee attaches to your account cleanly.
  2. Mark the 30-day line in your calendar. The clock starts at purchase. Test the speed, support and the renewal figure during that window.
  3. Keep the domain separate in your head. If you took a "free domain," know that the registration may not come back if you refund — the hosting fee is the part the guarantee is built around.
  4. Cancel via official support inside the window. Open a ticket / chat and request the refund within 30 days; don't wait for a renewal to post.
  5. Confirm what was refunded. Check that the hosting plan fee was returned and note any separate items (domain, add-ons) the terms excluded.

Where DealDrop, HotDeals and TrulyCodes fall short

The gap this article covers is factual, not a grudge. As of our review (2026-10-17), the three benchmark aggregators optimise their A2 Hosting / hosting.com pages for one thing — a list of codes and a headline percentage-off — and they almost uniformly skip the money-back mechanics:

  • They sell the code, not the exit. The pages are built to get you to copy a promo string; the 30-day guarantee that actually de-risks your purchase is rarely placed next to it.
  • They don't separate "plan" from "domain" and "add-ons." A reader can finish a coupon page believing the whole order is refundable, when the domain and add-ons are typically separate and often excluded.
  • They don't flag the renewal link. The guarantee matters most as protection against a renewal-price shock — the exact thing the aggregators also tend to leave out of their "% off" frames.
  • They imply the code is "risk-free." Risk-free is the guarantee's job, not the coupon's; conflating the two hides the one real safety net.

That is the gap: searchers want to know "if this deal goes bad, can I get my money back, and what's excluded?" — and the aggregators optimise for the code, not that answer.
