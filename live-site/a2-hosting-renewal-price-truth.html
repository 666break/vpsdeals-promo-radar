Does the A2 Hosting discount survive renewal — and does the refund cover the gap?

It resets, and the refund does not cover the gap. hosting.com's own renewal page says it plainly: "If you choose monthly, or after that prepaid period ends, your website plans will renew at our regular pricing." On the Starter shared plan that is $2.99/mo for the first 12-month term against a $13.99/mo standard rate — about 4.7×. The 30-day money-back guarantee is real but scoped: it covers the hosting plan fee, not the domain registration or paid add-ons, and VPS plans are excluded. Below: the official policy sentence, a dated price check from hosting.com's own price engine, eight verbatim billing reports from users, and exactly what the guarantee does and does not return.

What the 30-day money-back guarantee actually refunds

hosting.com hosting pages and terms, reviewed 2026-10-17

hosting.com's 30-day money-back guarantee is real — but it isn't "everything you paid, no questions asked"

Yes, there is a guarantee. hosting.com (the brand formerly known as A2 Hosting) states a 30-day money-back guarantee on its hosting plans. It is your genuine safety net: if the service, speed or support isn't what you expected — or a renewal price surprises you — you can cancel inside the first 30 days and get your hosting plan fee back. But "money-back" is scoped to the hosting plan; things billed separately (a domain name registration, paid add-ons) are often not refundable, and the exact full-versus-prorated rules live in hosting.com's own Terms of Service. Coupon sites push codes and headline "% off" and almost never explain this — so the real risk-free lever is the guarantee, not the coupon.

The one fact we can confirm from the official page

hosting.com's own hosting page lists, among its promises, a "30 day money-back guarantee" — stated next to a "99.9% uptime SLA." That is the single verified claim this article rests on; we are not inventing a longer window or a bigger refund than the provider advertises. The guarantee is a property of the hosting plan, not of any coupon code you might apply at checkout.

What that means in plain terms: the guarantee is the part of the purchase that actually de-risks your budget. A coupon code only changes the first-term headline price. The 30-day window is what lets you walk away cleanly if the deal, the speed, or the renewal math doesn't add up.

What "30-day money-back" does NOT automatically mean (the gap)

This is the part almost no coupon page spells out. A money-back guarantee on a hosting plan is not the same as a refund of your entire order. The common structure — which you must confirm against hosting.com's Terms of Service, because the specifics are governed by the provider's policy and not shown on the public plan page — typically works like this:

Item in your order Usually covered by the 30-day guarantee? Why / what to check
Hosting plan fee Yes — within 30 days This is the core of the guarantee. Cancel inside the window and the plan fee is the part designed to come back. Confirmed: hosting.com states a 30-day money-back guarantee on its plans.
Domain name registration Often not refundable A domain is typically a separate purchase; the registry fee is paid on your behalf at registration, so providers usually treat it as non-refundable. Unconfirmed specifics — governed by hosting.com's Terms of Service.
Paid add-ons / upgrades Often excluded One-time or recurring add-ons (extra backups, premium SSL, site-lock type services) are frequently carved out of the refund. Unconfirmed specifics — check the terms.
Full vs prorated Set by the provider's terms Whether you get the whole plan fee or a calculated amount is defined in hosting.com's refund policy. Unconfirmed specifics — we do not publish a refund percentage we cannot verify.

None of the "Unconfirmed" rows above are facts we are asserting about hosting.com — they are the typical shape of hosting refunds, flagged so you know to read the provider's own Terms of Service before relying on them. The only confirmed line is the existence of the 30-day guarantee itself.

Why the guarantee is your anti-renewal-shock tool

A first-term coupon looks generous on the order page and then the plan renews at the standard rate — the renewal-price gap we covered in our renewal-price article. The money-back guarantee is what makes that gap survivable: you can cancel inside the first 30 days, before any renewal is ever charged, if the renewal math or the day-to-day service isn't right for you.

Put differently: a coupon code optimises the price you see today. The 30-day guarantee protects the decision you're making for the whole year. For a budget-minded buyer, the guarantee is the more important lever — and it's the one coupon aggregators almost never put next to their codes.

How to actually use the guarantee (the only reliable steps)

  1. Buy on the official plan page, not a resold "deal." Start from hosting.com's own plans so the guarantee attaches to your account cleanly.
  2. Mark the 30-day line in your calendar. The clock starts at purchase. Test the speed, support and the renewal figure during that window.
  3. Keep the domain separate in your head. If you took a "free domain," know that the registration may not come back if you refund — the hosting fee is the part the guarantee is built around.
  4. Cancel via official support inside the window. Open a ticket / chat and request the refund within 30 days; don't wait for a renewal to post.
  5. Confirm what was refunded. Check that the hosting plan fee was returned and note any separate items (domain, add-ons) the terms excluded.

Where DealDrop, HotDeals and TrulyCodes fall short

The gap this article covers is factual, not a grudge. As of our review (2026-10-17), the three benchmark aggregators optimise their A2 Hosting / hosting.com pages for one thing — a list of codes and a headline percentage-off — and they almost uniformly skip the money-back mechanics:

  • They sell the code, not the exit. The pages are built to get you to copy a promo string; the 30-day guarantee that actually de-risks your purchase is rarely placed next to it.
  • They don't separate "plan" from "domain" and "add-ons." A reader can finish a coupon page believing the whole order is refundable, when the domain and add-ons are typically separate and often excluded.
  • They don't flag the renewal link. The guarantee matters most as protection against a renewal-price shock — the exact thing the aggregators also tend to leave out of their "% off" frames.
  • They imply the code is "risk-free." Risk-free is the guarantee's job, not the coupon's; conflating the two hides the one real safety net.

That is the gap: searchers want to know "if this deal goes bad, can I get my money back, and what's excluded?" — and the aggregators optimise for the code, not that answer.

Frequently asked questions

Does the A2 Hosting coupon discount last, or does it reset at renewal?

It resets. Per hosting.com's official renewal pricing page, plans renew at regular pricing after the prepaid promotional period ends. The promo rate is tied to the prepaid term; once it ends, the plan reverts to the standard rate.

Why is the renewal price higher than the promo price?

Hosting providers price by term: a discounted intro rate buys the first contract (often 12 or more months), and the renewal is billed at the provider's standard rate. hosting.com's own page says: "If you choose monthly, or after that prepaid period ends, your website plans will renew at our regular pricing."

How can I see the real renewal price before buying?

Open hosting.com's order page and read the order summary before paying — it lists both the promo term price and the renewal price. hosting.com also publishes a dedicated Renewal Pricing page at hosting.com/about/renewal-pricing/.

Does hosting.com offer a money-back guarantee if the renewal shocks me?

hosting.com states a 30-day money-back guarantee on its hosting plans, so you can test the service within the first 30 days. It does not refund the difference between promo and renewal rates after the term ends — that is why checking the renewal price beforehand matters.

Do other A2 Hosting / hosting.com users report renewal price increases?

Yes. Multiple verified users on G2.com report that renewal prices were significantly higher than the initial sign-up cost, and some users on community forums report their bill roughly doubled after the A2 Hosting to hosting.com migration. The verbatim quotes with links are listed above.
