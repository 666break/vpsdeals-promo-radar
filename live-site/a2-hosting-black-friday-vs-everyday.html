The "always-on promo" is the baseline, not the exception

Most hosting guides write about Black Friday as if the rest of the year is "full price." That framing breaks for providers like hosting.com, which display a promotional rate at order every single day. The promo isn't a November-only event you have to wait for — it is the normal state of the plan page. So the real question isn't "will November be cheaper?" but "is November cheaper than the discount that's already live?"

That reframing changes the math. If the everyday promo already takes the same percentage off the same plan, a Black Friday banner that advertises "the biggest discount of the year" may be showing you the identical number you could lock in today. The thing that genuinely changes your long-run cost isn't the first-term headline — it's the renewal rate, and Black Friday rarely touches that.

Why Black Friday looks bigger than it is

A few mechanics make a November banner feel like a once-a-year steal, even when the underlying number is unchanged:

  • Urgency framing. Countdown timers and "ends Sunday" language push you to buy now. Urgency is a sales tactic, not evidence the price is lower than yesterday's.
  • Relabeled discount. The same plan, the same percentage off, a new badge. The cart total for a given term can be identical to a non-event week.
  • The "best deal" needs a longer term. The lowest per-month number during BF often requires prepaying 24 or 36 months. That's a commitment decision, not a discount that disappears in December.
  • Renewal stays put. hosting.com states plans "renew at our regular pricing" after the first prepaid period — a sale banner doesn't usually lower what you pay from year two onward.

The decision logic: when waiting pays, and when it doesn't

This is the part coupon roundups skip — they want you to wait and click, not to decide. Here is the actual trade-off:

  • If you need hosting now: an always-on promo provider means you can lock the discounted rate today. Waiting until November to "save" can cost you months of live service at a price that may be identical. The launch you delay is a real cost; the discount you're chasing may not be.
  • If you won't launch until after the holidays: a genuine BF extra (a longer discount window or a bundled add-on) is free upside — as long as you verify the cart total and the renewal rate first.
  • Watch the renewal, not just the headline: the first-term percentage is what every banner screams about, but the renewal rate is what you pay for years. A "bigger" BF first-term discount that renews higher can be worse overall than a smaller everyday promo with a lower renewal.
  • The term trap: the lowest per-month price almost always means committing longer upfront. That's fine if you're sure, but it's a commitment, not a November-only magic number.

Where DealDrop, HotDeals and TrulyCodes fall short

The gap this article covers is factual, not a grudge. As of our review (2026-10-15), the three benchmark aggregators tend to do the following on their A2 Hosting / hosting.com deal pages:

  • They manufacture BF urgency. Dedicated "Black Friday A2 Hosting deals" and "best BF coupons" pages push you to wait and click, often without ever comparing the BF rate to the everyday promo rate like-for-like.
  • They rarely do the like-for-like check. Showing a BF percentage next to a "regular" price implies a saving, but if the everyday promo already matches that percentage, the comparison is misleading. The side-by-side cart math is the missing piece.
  • They show the headline % off, not the renewal rate. A first-term Black Friday "70% off" looks enormous until you see what the plan renews at — and that renewal figure is usually absent from the deal page.
  • They don't tell you the promo is already live. A reader is left thinking they must wait for November, when hosting.com's plan page shows a promotional rate at order every day. The timing decision is never framed honestly.

That is the gap: searchers want to know "is Black Friday actually cheaper, or should I just buy now?" — and the aggregators are built to make you wait and click, not to give you that answer.
