Is there a domain promo code — and what actually moves the price?

Conclusion: no standing public promo code is printed on any of the three official registrar pages we opened on 2026-10-27 — Porkbun's domain pricing page at porkbun.com/products/domains, IONOS's page at ionos.com/domains/domain-names, and Cloudflare Registrar's page at cloudflare.com/products/registrar. How we checked: we fetched each page's full source on that date and searched it for “promo code”, “coupon code” and “referral”. “promo code” returned 0 hits on all three. “coupon code” returned 3 hits, all on Porkbun's cart page, where the phrase labels the code-entry field. “referral” returned 1 hit, in IONOS's footer, as a link labelled “Refer a Friend”. None of the three hits is a code. Coverage: those three pages, read on 2026-10-27, and nothing beyond them.

What does move the price is printed on the same pages, and it is not a code — it is the first-year figure set against the renewal figure. Both are listed side by side below, exactly as published:

  • Porkbun prints both numbers. Read 2026-10-27: .com $11.08 registration and $11.08 renewal; .net $12.52 and $12.52; .org $7.98 and $11.84; .io $28.12 and $51.80; .xyz $2.04 and $14.21. The registration figure carries a “1st Yr Sale!” badge on .org, .io, .app, .xyz and .dev — not on .com or .net.
  • IONOS prints a first-year figure and a struck-through price, but no after-price. Read 2026-10-27: .com $1 per year for 1 year, with $20 shown struck through; .io $40.99, with $90 struck through. The page's own price block carries [after:false] and its after-price section is empty.
  • Cloudflare Registrar prints a policy, not per-extension numbers. Its page states that registration, transfer and renewal prices are always at or below what registries and ICANN charge it, and that it does not mark up domain prices at all.

How to check any registrar yourself, in three steps

  1. Open the registrar's own domain-pricing page — not a coupon site, and not the search box on its home page.
  2. Find the row for your extension and read two numbers, not one: what the first term costs, and what applies afterwards. If the page prints only one number, treat the second year as unknown rather than assuming it matches the first.
  3. Read the page source as well as the page. Porkbun prints its two prices in the attributes data-price-registration and data-price-renewal in US cents; those attributes are what the page's own script uses, so they are the figure to trust if the visible text and the attribute ever disagree.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a domain promo code that works at every registrar?

Not on any of the three official registrar pages we opened on 2026-10-27. We fetched the full source of Porkbun's domain pricing page, IONOS's domain-names page and Cloudflare Registrar's product page and searched each for “promo code”, “coupon code” and “referral”. “promo code” returned zero hits on all three. “coupon code” returned three hits, all on Porkbun's cart page, where the phrase labels the code-entry field. “referral” returned one hit, in IONOS's footer, as a link labelled “Refer a Friend”. None of the three hits is a code. This finding covers those three pages on that date only.

Does Porkbun have a promo code?

No standing public promo code is printed on Porkbun's own pages. What Porkbun prints instead is a first-year price and a separate renewal price for each extension, and on several extensions the first-year price carries a “1st Yr Sale!” badge. If you are holding a code that Porkbun refuses at checkout, Porkbun promo code — the rejection message word for word, and where the code field is quotes the fixed message its cart page prints and names the five cases in it.

Why is a domain renewal more expensive than the first year?

Because the registrar prints two different numbers for the same extension. On Porkbun's pricing page, read 2026-10-27, .io is listed at $28.12 for registration and $51.80 for renewal, and .xyz at $2.04 for registration and $14.21 for renewal. On .com and .net the two numbers are the same: $11.08 and $12.52. The difference is a published price, not a hidden fee, and no code is involved in either figure.

Does Cloudflare Registrar charge more at renewal?

Cloudflare's Registrar page, read 2026-10-27, states that “Registration, transfer, and renewal prices are always at or below what registries and ICANN charge us”, and its FAQ states that Cloudflare Registrar does not mark up domain prices at all. That page does not print a per-extension price list, so we cannot quote a .com or .io figure from it and do not.

For a .com, is Porkbun or IONOS cheaper?

For the first year, IONOS prints $1 (with $20 shown struck through and the offer text “for 1 year”), and Porkbun prints $11.08. For renewal, Porkbun prints $11.08, the same number as its registration price. IONOS does not print a renewal or after-price figure on the page we read, so we cannot state its second-year cost and do not.

Where do I see the renewal price before I buy?

On the registrar's own domain-pricing page, and read two numbers rather than one. Porkbun prints both in visible text and again in the page's own attributes data-price-registration and data-price-renewal, in US cents. IONOS prints the first-year price and a struck-through price with no after-price section on that page, so for IONOS the second-year figure is unknown until the cart or the contract states it.
