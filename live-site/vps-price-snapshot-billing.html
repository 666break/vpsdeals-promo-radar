VPS Snapshot Cost: What a Snapshot Bills After You Take It

By Marcus Reid · Published Oct 31, 2026 · Last updated Oct 31, 2026 · 9 min read

A snapshot is a storage line item, not a feature. The three providers publish it three different ways: DigitalOcean at $0.06 per GiB per month with a $0.01 minimum, Hetzner at €0.0143 per GB per month excluding VAT and billed only on the compressed size, and OVHcloud as a flat “from $0.40 per month” with no per-GB rate published at all.

A snapshot is one line of the bill — what the monthly bill looks like with snapshots prices the other five.

  • DigitalOcean prices the snapshot's size, Hetzner prices its compressed size. Same unit name, different quantity being measured — which is why the two list rates cannot be compared by reading the numbers alone.
  • A full-disk snapshot on DigitalOcean's Basic ladder adds $0.60 to $19.20 per month, depending on which plan you bought. The cheapest plan carries the smallest snapshot bill and the largest plans carry the largest, but the figures do not rise in step with the plan price.
  • On four of the seven Basic plans, the full-disk snapshot lands on exactly the same dollar figure as the weekly percentage backup — $3.60 on the $18.00 plan, $4.80 on the $24.00 plan, $9.60 on the $48.00 plan, $19.20 on the $96.00 plan. On the 25 GiB and 50 GiB plans the snapshot figure is higher ($1.50 against $1.20, $3.00 against $2.40); on the 10 GiB entry plan it is lower ($0.60 against $0.80).
  • Hetzner's own example turns the list rate into a ceiling. A 40 GB disk is billed at “40GB or less”, and the same server's backup is a flat 20% of the server price — €0.572 for the snapshot against €1.198 for the backup on a €5.99 instance.
  • OVHcloud is the only one of the three that deletes the snapshot for you. Its options page says a restored snapshot “is automatically deleted”, so the restore is not the end of the cost — re-creating the snapshot starts the meter again.

Every VPS price page in this niche lists snapshots as a bullet point. Almost none of them put a number next to it, and the number is the only part that shows up on an invoice. This page collects the three published rates we could read on 2026-10-31, states the unit each one is billed in, and does the arithmetic that turns a rate into a monthly line item. Where a provider's page does not answer a question — whether a deleted server stops the snapshot charge at DigitalOcean, for instance — this article says so rather than filling the gap.

Two providers, two rates that look comparable per gigabyte, two different quantities on the meter.

Where a Snapshot and a Weekly Backup Land on the Same Number

This is the part that is genuinely surprising, and it falls straight out of the two published rate structures. DigitalOcean sells backups two ways: as a percentage of the Droplet's monthly cost (20% weekly, 30% daily) or as a usage-based per-GiB rate. The 20% is not phrased as a sentence on that page — it is a row in the Basic Plans table, under the lead-in “Basic plans add the following costs to your Droplet:”, and the row reads “Weekly — 20.0% of the Droplet’s cost per month”, with the daily row reading “30.0% of the Droplet’s cost per month”. The prose above the table says: “Basic plans add a flat percentage of the Droplet’s cost to the monthly bill, while usage-based plans add a flat rate per GiB of a backup’s restorable file size.” Source: docs.digitalocean.com/products/backups/details/pricing/, page marked last_updated: 2026-03-09, read on 2026-10-31. The percentage form scales with the price of the Droplet; the snapshot rate scales with the size of the disk. On the four plans from $18.00 upward those two scales produce identical dollar amounts:

  • $18.00 Droplet, 60 GiB disk: snapshot 60 × $0.06 = $3.60; weekly backup 20% of $18.00 = $3.60.
  • $24.00 Droplet, 80 GiB disk: snapshot 80 × $0.06 = $4.80; weekly backup 20% of $24.00 = $4.80.
  • $48.00 Droplet, 160 GiB disk: snapshot 160 × $0.06 = $9.60; weekly backup 20% of $48.00 = $9.60.
  • $96.00 Droplet, 320 GiB disk: snapshot 320 × $0.06 = $19.20; weekly backup 20% of $96.00 = $19.20.

That coincidence is arithmetic, not a promotion. It holds on the four rows where the disk and the price move together — 60 GiB at $18.00, 80 GiB at $24.00, 160 GiB at $48.00, 320 GiB at $96.00 — because on those rows 20% of the Droplet price and $0.06 on every gigabyte of its disk resolve to the same number. On the 25 GiB and 50 GiB plans the disk is larger relative to the price, so the snapshot comes out above the weekly backup ($1.50 against $1.20, $3.00 against $2.40). On the 10 GiB entry plan it comes out below ($0.60 against $0.80).

The practical consequence: on a mid-size Droplet, an on-demand snapshot you keep indefinitely costs the same per month as the weekly automated backup — but it is one image, at one point in time, with no schedule and no retention policy behind it. What you are buying with the snapshot is control over when it is taken and how long it lives; what you are buying with the backup is repetition. The prices do not distinguish between those two things, and the price list does not tell you which one your workload needs.

The usage-based backup rate is the cheaper meter

The same DigitalOcean backups page lists a second, non-percentage structure for plans that use it: $0.04 per GiB per month for weekly, $0.03 for daily, $0.02 for every 12 hours, $0.015 for every 6 hours and $0.01 for every 4 hours. Set the daily figure beside the snapshot rate and the ordering is plain: $0.06 per GiB-month for a snapshot you take by hand, $0.03 per GiB-month for a backup taken daily and kept on a schedule. Both are per-GiB meters on the same account, and the manual one is priced higher. The page does not explain why, and this article will not invent a reason.

Hetzner Bills the Compressed Size, and That Makes the Rate a Ceiling

Hetzner's add-on block states the rate plainly: snapshots are €0.0143 per GB per month, excluding VAT. Its billing FAQ then explains what the GB is: not the disk, but “the space the compressed Snapshot uses in our storage system”. The example it gives is a 40 GB disk billed at “40GB or less”. So €0.0143 is the maximum per gigabyte, and the real figure depends on how well your data compresses.

Put that against the same server's backup, which Hetzner prices completely differently: “a monthly flat price, which is 20% of the price of the server you activate them for”, and that flat price buys seven backup slots. Hetzner's own worked example uses a €3.29 server, where the backup is €3.29 × 0.2 = €0.658 per month. Run the same formula on the Cost-Optimized CX23 we read on 2026-10-31 — 2 vCPUs, 4 GB RAM, 40 GB NVMe, €5.99 per month excluding VAT — and the backup is €5.99 × 0.2 = €1.198 per month. That is exactly the figure the same page prints as the backup add-on's starting price, which is a useful cross-check: the “starting at €1.1980” on the marketing page is the 20% formula applied to the cheapest instance.

One asymmetry is worth naming, because it changes how you should read the two numbers. A backup at Hetzner is priced off the server, so it rises when you upgrade the server and stays flat when your data grows. A snapshot is priced off the data, so it rises when your disk fills and stays flat when you upgrade the CPU. For a database that grows steadily, the flat 20% is the predictable line; for a machine that gets rebuilt and resized often, the per-GB meter is.

OVHcloud Sells the Snapshot as a Named Option, and Deletes It on Restore

OVHcloud is the odd one out in two ways. First, it does not publish a per-GB rate at all: the VPS options page gives three tiers with “starting at” monthly prices — standard automatic backup included with a 24-hour window, automatic backup with 7 rolling days from $1.40 per month, and a manual backup from $0.40 per month. Without a per-GB figure there is no way to compute what a 40 GB snapshot costs on VPS-1; the page simply does not expose the arithmetic, and we are not going to estimate it.

Second, and more consequential, the page describes what happens after a restore: “Once restored, it is automatically deleted.” A snapshot at OVHcloud is single-use. You take it before an upgrade, you restore it if the upgrade goes wrong, and then it is gone — so the next upgrade needs a new one. That is a different product from DigitalOcean's snapshot, which sits in your account until you delete it, and from Hetzner's, which the FAQ says “remain permanently stored until they are actively deleted”. Same word on three price lists, three different lifetimes.

What the OVHcloud page does not say. It gives no per-GB rate, no size tiers, no statement about whether a short-lived manual backup is prorated, and no statement about what happens to the charge if the VPS is deleted while the backup still exists. Those four gaps are left as gaps here. A “from $0.40” figure is a floor, not a price, and treating it as one would make the comparison table above look far tidier than the source material allows.

Does the Snapshot Outlive the Server?

This is the question that decides whether a snapshot is a cheap way to park a machine or a bill you forget about. Hetzner answers it directly: snapshots “remain intact even after the original server has been deleted” and stay until deleted. That is the same page that recommends snapshotting and deleting a server as the cheaper alternative to leaving it switched off — advice that only makes sense if the snapshot keeps billing on its own meter after the server is gone.

DigitalOcean's snapshot pricing page does not address the question. It says pricing is based on the size of the snapshot, that the minimum charge is $0.01, and that there is “no additional charge for making a snapshot available in multiple regions”. Nothing about server deletion. We are not going to assert an answer on DigitalOcean's behalf; if this matters to your bill, the safe assumption is the conservative one, and the way to settle it is to delete a test Droplet with a snapshot attached and read the next invoice.

This connects to the wider question of what a powered-off server costs, which we covered separately: at all three providers a stopped server keeps billing, and the snapshot is not a way to avoid that charge — it is a way to replace the server entirely. The economics only work if the snapshot costs less per month than the idle server it replaces, which is exactly the comparison Hetzner's FAQ is making and exactly the comparison OVHcloud's single-use snapshot cannot support.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a VPS snapshot cost per month?

DigitalOcean charges $0.06 per GiB per month for a Droplet snapshot, with a minimum charge of $0.01. Hetzner charges €0.0143 per GB per month excluding VAT, and bills only the space the compressed snapshot occupies. OVHcloud does not publish a per-GB rate: its VPS options page lists a manual snapshot as starting at $0.40 per month and a seven-day automatic backup as starting at $1.40 per month, with the 24-hour standard backup included. All three figures were read from the providers' own pages on 2026-10-31.

Is a snapshot cheaper than an automatic backup?

It depends on the plan shape, and DigitalOcean publishes both rates on the same page so the comparison can be made directly. A snapshot costs $0.06 per GiB per month; the percentage-based backup adds 20 percent of the Droplet's monthly cost for weekly backups or 30 percent for daily ones. On the $24.00 Droplet with an 80 GiB disk, a full-disk snapshot is $4.80, the weekly backup adds $4.80 and the daily backup adds $7.20. On the $6.00 Droplet with 25 GiB, the snapshot is $1.50, the weekly backup adds $1.20 and the daily backup adds $1.80.

Do I keep paying for a snapshot after I delete the server?

Hetzner states it explicitly: snapshots remain intact even after the original server has been deleted, and they stay stored until you actively delete them. DigitalOcean's snapshot pricing page does not address the question either way, so this article does not claim an answer for DigitalOcean. What that page does say is that pricing is based on the size of the snapshot and that there is no additional charge for making a snapshot available in multiple regions.

Is a snapshot that exists for only a few days billed in full?

Hetzner says yes to prorating: if a snapshot exists for only a fraction of a month, it bills for that fraction. DigitalOcean does not state a proration rule. Its page says pricing is based on the size of the snapshot and that a minimum charge of $0.01 may apply for very small snapshots or snapshots that exist only for a short time, which describes a time-based floor without stating the proration formula. OVHcloud's options page gives a monthly starting price and does not describe short-lived snapshots.

Bottom Line

A snapshot is priced per gigabyte per month, and the gigabyte means something different at each provider: the size of the image at DigitalOcean, the compressed size in storage at Hetzner, an unnamed quantity behind a “from” figure at OVHcloud. That single difference explains why the three rates cannot be ranked by reading them. What can be ranked is the effect on a real bill, and there DigitalOcean publishes enough to do the arithmetic: a full-disk snapshot adds $0.60 to $19.20 a month across the Basic range, and on the four plans from $18.00 upward that figure lands on exactly the same dollar amount as the weekly percentage backup. A snapshot is not a free feature you switch on. It is a second copy of the disk, billed by the gigabyte, that outlives the server that made it.
