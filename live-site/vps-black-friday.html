What Black Friday VPS Deals Actually Look Like

Across the budget and mid-tier hosts, Black Friday VPS promotions share a shape:

  • A tiny headline box. A 512 MB–1 GB KVM plan at a eye-catching annual rate is the hook. It is real, but it's the same floor you get on the standing promo — sometimes a touch cheaper, rarely dramatically so.
  • Discounted mid tiers. The actual value is usually in the 1–4 GB range, where annual-promo rates drop for the event window. This is where waiting for BF can pay off if you need more than a toy box.
  • Annual prepay required. Every "deal" is an annual term. The low monthly figure only holds if you pay for a year — the same structure as the standing promos, just occasionally a bit lower.
  • KVM, not containers. Reputable BF VPS deals are KVM (true isolation). Be wary of OpenVZ/LXC "deals" at rock-bottom prices — shared kernel, restricted, and often oversold.

The managed hosts (DigitalOcean, Vultr) tend to discount their higher-tier and product bundles rather than the cheapest entry, while the budget KVM specialists (RackNerd, Contabo, OVHcloud) discount the core VPS lineup directly. Know which camp your target is in before judging the "deal."

The Gap Most "VPS Black Friday" Articles Miss

The typical VPS Black Friday page is an affiliate link dump posted months early with unverified prices and a fake "list price" to make the discount look huge. Both vendor pages (which we exclude from benchmarking because they only show who owns the head term) and the affiliate tables miss what actually protects your wallet:

  • (1) No standing-promo benchmark. Without the verified year-round price, you can't tell a real discount from a relabeled standing promo. We anchor everything to the verified baselines above.
  • (2) Fake list prices. A "$48/mo slashed to $3/mo" is meaningless if the real standing price is already $3. Judge against the provider's actual promo, not its MSRP.
  • (3) The 512 MB floor. The headline BF box is tiny. They don't say it's the same floor as the standing promo and unfit for real workloads.
  • (4) RAM-per-dollar blindness. A $3/512 MB "deal" loses to a $5/8 GB standing plan. The dollar figure is a distraction from the metric that matters.
  • (5) Windows licensing. Budget KVM "Windows VPS" BF deals bill the license separately, so the promo price isn't the real cost. All-in Windows is Contabo/IONOS, not a $2 KVM box.

How do I avoid a bad Black Friday VPS deal?
