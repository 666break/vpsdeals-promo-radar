DigitalOcean vs Vultr vs Hostinger: Which VPS Hosting Wins in 2026

By Marcus Reid · Published Sep 23, 2026 · Last updated Sep 23, 2026 · 7 min read

The Verdict (Read This First)

  • Cheapest of the three: Vultr. Regular Performance starts at $2.50/month (IPv6-only) and $5/month buys 1 vCPU, 1 GB RAM, 25 GB SSD and 1 TB. It is also the only one of the three with hourly billing.
  • Most RAM per dollar at entry: Hostinger. The KVM 1 promo is $6.49/month for 1 vCPU, 4 GB RAM, 50 GB NVMe and 4 TB — four times the RAM of Vultr's $5 plan. The catch: it renews at $11.99/month.
  • Best developer ecosystem: DigitalOcean. Clean API, one-click apps, and the deepest docs. Its Premium shared Droplet at $7/month (1 vCPU, 1 GB, 25 GB NVMe, 1 TB) matches Vultr's $5 plan on specs but costs more.
  • Pick by who you are: solo dev who lives in the terminal → DigitalOcean or Vultr; absolute lowest bill → Vultr; beginner who wants a control panel and hand-holding → Hostinger.
  • Reality check: none of these three is the cheapest VPS on earth. RackNerd and Hetzner go lower (see our cheap VPS guide) — but among the three mainstream brands, Vultr is the price leader and Hostinger the RAM leader.

If you type "vps hosting" into a search engine, three names dominate the results: DigitalOcean, Vultr, and Hostinger. They are not the three cheapest providers in the world, but they are the three you are most likely to actually consider. Detailed Breakdown

Vultr — The Price Leader

From $2.50/mo (IPv6) · $5/mo = 1 vCPU / 1 GB / 25 GB SSD / 1 TB · hourly billing from $0.004/hr

Vultr wins the headline price war. Its Regular Performance tier starts at $2.50/month for an IPv6-only instance with 0.5 GB RAM and 10 GB of storage, and $5/month gets you a 1 GB / 25 GB plan that is directly comparable to DigitalOcean's $7 Premium tier — for two dollars less. Vultr also offers hourly billing, which is genuinely useful if you spin up a server for a few hours of testing and tear it down: you pay for the hours you use, not a full month.

The trade-off is the $2.50 plan itself: 512 MB of RAM is too little for almost anything modern, and IPv6-only means you need IPv6 connectivity to reach it. The $5 plan is the realistic floor for real work. Vultr also publishes the widest geographic spread of the three, with locations across North America, Europe, Asia, and Australia.

Pros

  • Lowest entry price of the three ($2.50/mo)
  • Hourly billing — pay only for hours used
  • Broad location network incl. Asia & Australia
  • Bare-metal option for heavy workloads

Cons

  • $2.50 plan is IPv6-only with 0.5 GB RAM
  • Unmanaged; ticket-based support
  • Fewer one-click apps than DigitalOcean

DigitalOcean — The Developer Default

Shared CPU from $4/mo · Premium $7/mo = 1 vCPU / 1 GB / 25 GB NVMe / 1 TB · hourly billing

DigitalOcean is the provider developers reach for by default, and the reason is the ecosystem, not the price. The API is clean, the one-click application library (Docker, LAMP, LEMP, MySQL, Node.js) is mature, and the documentation is the best in the business. If you want to script your infrastructure or follow a tutorial written by someone else, DigitalOcean is where the examples live.

On pure specs it is the most expensive of the three at comparable tiers: the $7 Premium plan matches Vultr's $5 plan exactly on paper (1 vCPU, 1 GB, 25 GB, 1 TB) but costs 40% more. You are paying for tooling and predictability. The $4 basic shared Droplet is the cheapest verified entry from a major provider, but 512 MiB of RAM is tight for anything beyond a static site or a toy app.

Pros

  • Best-in-class docs, API, and one-click apps
  • Predictable, transparent pricing
  • Large community and tutorial base
  • Global datacenter coverage

Cons

  • $7 Premium plan costs more than Vultr's matching $5
  • 512 MiB on the $4 plan is tight
  • Unmanaged (Cloudways resells it managed)

Hostinger — The Beginner-Friendly Pick

KVM 1 promo $6.49/mo (renews $11.99) · 1 vCPU / 4 GB / 50 GB NVMe / 4 TB · range $6.49–$25.99/mo

Hostinger is the RAM king at the entry level. For $6.49/month you get 4 GB of RAM and 50 GB of NVMe storage — four times the RAM of Vultr's or DigitalOcean's $5–$7 plans. It also throws in hPanel (its own control panel) and 24/7 support, which lowers the activation energy for someone who has never touched a terminal. Datacenters span four continents: North America, Europe, Asia, and South America.

The catch is the renewal: the $6.49 price is promotional and climbs to $11.99/month on a two-year term, and Hostinger bills monthly only — no hourly metering. For a beginner who wants a panel and a support chat, that is a fair trade. For a cost-obsessed developer, it is the worst long-term value of the three.

Pros

  • 4 GB RAM at entry — most of the three
  • hPanel + 24/7 support for beginners
  • 50 GB NVMe and 4 TB bandwidth
  • Four-continent datacenter coverage

Cons

  • Renews at $11.99/mo (2-yr term)
  • Monthly billing only, no hourly
  • Fewer raw-cloud features than DO/Vultr

So Which One Do You Actually Buy?

The three providers occupy different points on the same spectrum, and the "winner" is whoever matches your profile:

You are a developer who lives in the terminal. DigitalOcean or Vultr. Pick Vultr if your priority is the lowest bill and hourly billing; pick DigitalOcean if you want the API, docs, and one-click apps to save you setup time. Both are unmanaged, so you are doing the sysadmin work either way.

You want the absolute lowest monthly cost. Vultr, at $5/month for a usable 1 GB plan (or $2.50 if you can live with IPv6-only and 512 MB). It is the price leader of the three, full stop.

You are a beginner who fears the command line. Hostinger. The 4 GB of RAM and the hPanel + 24/7 support combination means you can get a site online without becoming a sysadmin. Just budget for the renewal price.

You want the best specs-per-dollar, period. Step outside this trio. As our cheap VPS hosting guide shows, Hetzner and RackNerd beat all three on raw value — but they trade away the brand recognition, location spread, and polish that DO, Vultr, and Hostinger provide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which of the three is the cheapest VPS hosting?

Vultr. Its Regular Performance tier starts at $2.50/month (IPv6-only, 0.5 GB RAM) or $5/month for a 1 GB / 25 GB plan. DigitalOcean's shared CPU starts at $4/month and Hostinger's KVM 1 promo is $6.49/month. None is the cheapest on the market overall, but Vultr is cheapest among these three.

Is Hostinger VPS managed or unmanaged?

Technically unmanaged KVM VPS, but it ships with hPanel and 24/7 support, making it far more beginner-friendly than raw DigitalOcean or Vultr. The downside is billing: monthly only, and the $6.49 promo renews at $11.99/month on a two-year term, with no hourly option.

Who should pick DigitalOcean over the other two?

Developers who value the ecosystem — clean API, one-click apps, deep docs, predictable pricing. You pay a premium ($7 vs Vultr's $5 at matching specs) for tooling and support maturity, not raw hardware.
